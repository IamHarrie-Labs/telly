Privacy

What Telly stores about you

Last updated 14 November 2026

What Telly does

Telly writes a set of social posts from your event details and schedules them through your Buffer account. When a detail changes, it shows you the corrections and applies only the ones you approve.

What Telly can access

You choose this on Buffer's consent screen. Telly asks for four permissions: read your account and channels (account:read), read your posts (posts:read), create and edit posts (posts:write), and stay connected without asking you to sign in every hour (offline_access). It does not read analytics, comments or anything outside Buffer.

What Telly stores

  • Your Buffer sign-in tokens stay in an encrypted cookie in your own browser. They are not saved in a database, unless you turn on watching (below).
  • Each campaign is saved in a database: the event details you typed, your Buffer account ID, the IDs of the Buffer posts Telly created, and the text, times and event card Telly last wrote to them. Telly needs this to notice when a post was edited by hand.
  • Request counts. To stop runaway scripts, Telly counts requests per minute from each internet address. It stores a scrambled, one-way version of the address, never the address itself, and clears the counts within minutes.
  • Error reports. If something breaks in the page or on the server, Telly records the error message and the page it happened on, so it can be fixed. These reports don't include your posts or event details.
  • Feedback you send through the feedback page is saved with your Buffer account ID, if you're signed in.

Telly has no analytics, advertising or tracking cookies and does not sell your data. Neon hosts the database and Vercel runs the app. The optional flyer and email services are described below.

Watching in the background (optional)

Watching is off unless you turn it on. When it's on, Telly moves your Buffer sign-in tokens from your browser cookie into its database, encrypted with a key only the server holds, so it can check your upcoming posts during scheduled background runs while you're away. If you give an email address, Telly saves it and uses it only to tell you when a post needs attention. Those emails are sent through Resend. Turning watching off, or using Delete my Telly data, deletes the stored tokens and the email address straight away.

The demo

The demo works without a Buffer account. Its campaigns and practice posts are kept in the same database under a random demo ID, never reach Buffer or any social network, and are deleted after seven days.

The event card

If you leave "Add this event card" ticked, Telly draws a picture of the event's title, date, time, venue and price and attaches it to each post. The picture isn't stored anywhere: its web address carries those details, signed so only Telly can make one, and the image is drawn when Buffer or a reader opens it. Anyone with the address can see the card, just as anyone can see the post it's on.

Listen

The Listen button uses your browser's built-in voice. Your browser handles the speech and the choice of voice. Telly does not receive the speech request.

Reading a flyer

If you upload a flyer, Telly sends the image to Anthropic's Claude API to read the event details. Telly does not save the image. You can type the details instead if you'd rather not upload a flyer.

Deleting your data

While connected, use Delete my Telly data at the bottom of the app to remove every campaign Telly saved for your account and turn off watching. Disconnect removes the sign-in cookie from your browser. Posts already scheduled in Buffer stay in Buffer; remove those there if you want them gone. You can also revoke Telly's access at any time from your Buffer account settings.
